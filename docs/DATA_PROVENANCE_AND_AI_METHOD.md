# ASTROLENZA v19 — Data Provenance & Analytical Method

## 1. Connected data sources

No external source is connected as a live feed in v19. All displayed crew and suit values, fault injections and mission events are generated by the local simulation. NASA sources are requirements references, research archives or future integration candidates—not feeds consumed by this visor.

### Runtime channel inventory

| Group | Channels | Meaning / qualification |
|---|---|---|
| Crew physiology | HR, RESP, SpO₂ | Synthetic heart rate, respiration and oxygen saturation |
| Helmet atmosphere | ppO₂, ppCO₂ | Synthetic oxygen and carbon-dioxide partial-pressure indicators |
| Suit integrity | PRESS | Synthetic internal suit pressure |
| Life support / thermal | FLOW, RPM, TSKIN, TOUT, BATT | Synthetic coolant flow, pump RPM, skin temperature, coolant temperature differential and battery state |
| Mission context | IMU, RAD | Synthetic movement load and radiation rate |
| Derived state | TCORE | Inferred thermal state, not an independent sensor channel |

There are 13 direct synthetic channels plus derived `TCORE`. Blood pressure (systolic/diastolic) is part of the broader longitudinal crew-health concept only; it is not a v19 runtime channel. Do not invent or display BP values as received telemetry.

## 2. AI and data analysis

The runtime uses a bounded model-based probabilistic inference pipeline, not a conversational LLM diagnosing astronauts.

1. **Signal simulation and quality:** generate time-varying channels with measurement noise and modeled fault injections.
2. **State estimation:** Kalman-style per-channel updates estimate a smoothed value, uncertainty, rate and innovation/residual.
3. **Consistency checks:** innovation gating and sensor-trust logic help distinguish physical changes from inconsistent or biased measurements.
4. **Fault classification:** compare residual patterns with candidate fault signatures using Gaussian likelihoods, then normalize nominal and fault hypotheses into Bayesian-style rankings.
5. **Conditional hazard projection:** project time-to-threshold only when an adverse trend persists, a warning band is occupied and the crossing lies inside the configured 30-minute horizon. Supported projections are pressure and thermal-margin cases.
6. **Next-best observation:** value-of-information / entropy-reduction logic ranks the channel expected to reduce diagnostic uncertainty.
7. **Human decision:** present the hypothesis, supporting signals and suggested measurement to the operator.

Posterior percentages are rankings over a synthetic candidate model, not calibrated field probabilities. Hazard projections are conditional indicators, not guaranteed countdowns. CO₂ contributes to threshold alerts and scrubber/fan fault reasoning, but the runtime does not currently produce a dedicated CO₂ time-to-hazard forecast.

## 3. Mission messages and operator control

| Action | System behavior | Fault-state effect |
|---|---|---|
| Attend | Selects affected crew, opens Suit view and focuses region | No automatic clearance |
| Escalate | Sets command priority and records escalation | Fault remains active |
| Resolve | Runs mapped simulated recovery | Simulated recovery / resolution state |
| Close | Ends message thread | Fault remains unchanged |
| Terminate | Ends message thread and records termination | Fault remains unchanged |

These controls do not command real suit hardware or crew systems.

## 4. NASA sources and intended use

### Standards and design requirements

- **NASA-STD-3001 Volume I — Crew Health, Revision C (2023-09-15):** https://standards.nasa.gov/node/238 — crew health, performance and medical-operations requirements context.
- **NASA-STD-3001 Volume II — Human Factors, Habitability and Environmental Health, Version F (2026-07-14):** https://standards.nasa.gov/standard/NASA/NASA-STD-3001_VOL_2 — human factors and environmental-health design context.
- **NASA Spacesuits, Volume II §11.3:** https://www.nasa.gov/reference/11-0-spacesuits-vol-2/ — suit atmospheric data recording, displaying, monitoring and alerting for suit pressure, ppO₂ and ppCO₂.

### Human research and architecture

- **NASA Human Research Program Evidence Library:** https://humanresearchroadmap.nasa.gov/evidence/ — risk and evidence context for crew health and performance on long-duration missions.
- **NASA Technical Reports Server, 20240004807 — Crew Health & Performance Integrated Data Architecture:** https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20240004807 — reference for integrated crew-health and performance data architecture.

### Research data and possible future interfaces

- **NASA Open Science Data Repository (OSDR):** https://science.nasa.gov/biological-physical/data/osdr/ — multimodal space-life-science research and metadata, including physiological, biomedical, behavioral and environmental telemetry. OSDR includes GeneLab and the Ames Life Sciences Data Archive (ALSDA). It supports research discovery and future validation, not live crew telemetry in this app.
- **OSDR Biological Data API:** https://visualization.osdr.nasa.gov/biodata/api/ — possible programmatic discovery interface. No active adapter in v19.
- **RadLab Data API:** https://visualization.osdr.nasa.gov/radlab/gui/data-api/ — radiation research data interface for future comparison and validation. No active adapter in v19.
- **NASA DONKI:** https://kauai.ccmc.gsfc.nasa.gov/DONKI/ — public space-weather event context that could inform mission exposure context. It is not a suit dosimeter and is not connected to this visor.

**Terminology:** NASA's Ames archive is the Ames Life Sciences Data Archive (**ALSDA**), integrated into OSDR. Older project material that used “LSDA” has been corrected in the v19 briefing.

## 5. Validation still required

- Connect only legally authorized and documented data interfaces; establish provenance, units, timestamps, calibration and missing-data handling.
- Curate labeled fault datasets and test sensor bias, drift, dropout, coupled faults and edge cases.
- Calibrate estimators, likelihoods, confidence and hazard horizons against representative ground-test and human-in-the-loop data.
- Validate physiology and suit thresholds with aerospace-medicine and systems-engineering experts.
- Add BP only after defining its sensor, acquisition cadence, quality flags, context and intended use.
- Complete independent safety, human-factors, cybersecurity, privacy and engineering review before operational claims.

## 6. Disclosure

ASTROLENZA v19 is a research prototype. It is not NASA-certified, medically validated, a medical device or approved for flight or operational decision-making. ChatGPT assisted source inspection, interface refinement, documentation, test design and the briefing update. Runtime reasoning is the local probabilistic model described above; no live conversational AI diagnosis service is used.
